DFRobot 6+1 mic array: ~$18
- Much cheaper than reSpeaker array
- Has more mics (7 total) than reSpeaker in same circular pattern
- I2S is reasonable
- 3 parallel stereo I2S interfaces with simul sampling
- No extra DSP chip: this just samples and passes the I2S lines

- Not very good documentation. But a once-responsive forum and some schematics probably give enough info to do that I need

The DFRobot 6+1 microphone array was selected over the Seeed Studio ReSpeaker 4-Mic Array primarily due to cost, raw hardware access, and educational value for embedded DSP development. Unlike the ReSpeaker arrays, which emphasize plug-and-play Linux integration and higher-level firmware abstraction, the DFRobot/Sipeed array exposes synchronized raw digital MEMS microphone outputs directly through multiple I2S data lanes sharing a common BCLK and WS clock. This architecture is better suited for implementing low-level DSP algorithms such as GCC-PHAT, delay-and-sum beamforming, and DOA estimation on an STM32H7 platform.
The board uses seven synchronized MSM261S4030H0 digital MEMS microphones arranged as three stereo I2S outputs plus one mono center microphone. Shared clocking across all microphones strongly suggests phase-aligned sampling, which is critical for beamforming and localization algorithms. The design appears to contain no onboard DSP or codec abstraction, meaning the STM32H7 firmware will directly manage multichannel audio capture, DMA buffering, synchronization, and processing.
Compared to the ReSpeaker ecosystem, the DFRobot board has substantially weaker documentation and software support, but offers significantly greater exposure to professional embedded audio pipeline design. This makes it a stronger fit for learning real-time embedded DSP architecture and developing transferable firmware engineering skills.
